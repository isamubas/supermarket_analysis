Meeting card · Retail analytics

Words, formulas and numbers

Mutundwe Family Supermarket — demonstration data
FY2025/26 · 3 branches · 354 SKUs · 1.3m lines
The one idea: every big number in this analysis is the same tiny sum — sold for minus cost — repeated a million times, then grouped differently. By product, by category, by branch, by hour. There is no clever maths anywhere.

The shape of the data

Line (line item)
One product on one receipt. Buy three things, you create three lines.
Ticket / basket
The whole receipt. One customer, one visit, one payment. Ours average 4.7 lines.
SKU
One product in one specific size. Sugar 1kg and sugar 2kg are two different SKUs — bought, priced and counted separately.

Money

Revenue / turnover
What the customer paid. Money in.
Cost of goods sold
What you paid the supplier for those same items. Goods only — not rent, wages or power.
Gross margin
Sale price minus what the goods cost you. The money the shop has before paying its bills.
Gross margin %
Margin as a share of the sale price. Says how hard each shilling of sales is working.
Average basket
Revenue divided by number of receipts. What a typical visit is worth.

Loss

Shrinkage
Stock you bought but never sold. Always split it into the next two — as one number, neither gets fixed.
Waste / spoilage
It rotted, expired or broke. An ordering and cold-chain problem.
Unexplained variance
Books say 50, shelf has 43, nothing recorded. A control problem — theft, sloppy counting, or goods never booked in.
Stockout
Shelf empty when a customer wanted the item.
Lost sales
Money you would have taken if it had not been empty. Invisible in the till — needs the daily stock file.

Stock & supply

Case pack
Suppliers sell cases, not units. A line selling two a month still arrives twelve at a time. This is what creates dead stock.
Days cover
How many days the stock on the shelf would last at the current selling rate.
Inventory days
Same idea for the whole shop. Ours: 10.6 days.
Fill rate
Of what you ordered, how much actually arrived. Our worst supplier: 76%.
Cash conversion cycle
Whether you pay the supplier before or after the customer pays you. Negative means the supplier is financing your stock — a strength.

Technique

KVI (known value item)
The few lines customers price from memory — sugar, oil, bread, soap, posho. They judge the whole shop on these.
Lift
How much more often two items share a basket than chance predicts. 1.0 = pure chance. 3.8 = a real habit.
Incremental margin
Whether a promotion left you with more money — not more units. Volume almost always rises; that proves nothing.
Supplier funding
The share of a promotion's discount the supplier rebates back. Decides whether the promo makes money. Rarely in the POS.

Compliance

Void
A cancelled sale at the till. Normal in small numbers — the rate is what matters.
EFRIS
URA's e-invoicing system. Unfiscalised tickets are a tax exposure, and a useful fraud signal.
VAT-exempt vs standard
Unprocessed foods sit outside VAT; packaged goods do not. Category margin means little until they are separated.

Before you trust any total — the five things that will make you wrong

  • Strip the voided tickets first. The line file has no void flag; you must join to the header. Worth UGX 122.7m of turnover that never happened.
  • Returns are negative lines. Sum quantity without checking the sign and both units and margin are wrong.
  • Airtime and Yaka are commission, not turnover. 4.3% of "sales", 0.8% of margin. Leave them in and every margin figure is distorted.
  • Use the cost in force on the day of sale, not today's cost — otherwise a price squeeze is invisible.
  • Stockouts leave no trace in the till. A sale that never happened has no row. You need the stock file.
